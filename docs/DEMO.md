# Demo script: eleven minutes for the Chief Officers Group

Audience: chief officers (council, health board, police), the CSWO, the Child and Adult Protection Committee chairs. Purpose: show what one shared picture of a person does for decisions, and that need-to-know is built in, not bolted on.

Three stories and two moments: Aiden Boyle (child protection), Kayleigh Docherty (MARAC with a linked child protection process), Marion Fraser (adult support and protection with a capacity concern), sixty seconds on a restricted MAPPA record seen by someone who is not on it, and sixty seconds on what the people hosting this could see if they looked. Two persona switches in total: Janet Kerr to Karen Findlay to Moira Gilmour.

Set-up (before the room fills): launch the Electron build (or `pnpm dev` and a browser at 1440 wide), sign in as Janet Kerr, set the theme to light and density to comfortable in Settings, and check the top bar shows "3 clocks due this week". ## Questions you will be asked, and the honest answers

- "Is this connected to anything?" No. Every adapter is a mock with fictional fixtures. The Connectors screen carries the "how this would connect for real" copy for each system.
- "Are the timescales right?" The statutory ones (28 days to a CPPM, first review within 6 months, 21 days for the MHO report, 12 months MARAC repeat window) are from national guidance and the Act. Local ones (ASP inquiry decision, ASP case conference, ASP plan review, MARAC research return, core group escalation) are configuration, marked "to verify" in the product and in `docs/RESEARCH.md`, and will be replaced with the Ayrshire values.
- "Who decided the need-to-know rules?" They are seeded from the brief's matrices and the national guidance, default deny, editable in Admin, and every change is audited. Where the guidance was silent, the more restrictive reading was chosen (`docs/DECISIONS.md`, D-042).
- "Can a perpetrator ever see anything?" No. Exclusions are keyed on the person's role on the case (perpetrator, perpetrator's associates, MAPPA victim), so they follow the record rather than a name, and the UI cannot lift them.
- "Who can break glass?" Only the Responsible Authority agencies, with a reason category and a written reason, for a window Admin sets (four hours by default). Every read inside the window is audited.
- "Why is there no OFFICIAL stamp on every page?" Because Annex 2 of the MAPPA National Guidance says there is no requirement to mark routine Official information, and a product that stamps every page teaches people to stop reading markings. Only Official-Sensitive is marked. The level is derived from what a record is about rather than chosen by whoever is printing; the rule table is in Admin, read-only, and anyone can raise a classification but only a named role can lower one.
- "Is it encrypted?" Record content is, end to end: each record is encrypted to exactly the people the need-to-know rules entitle, and whoever hosts the platform holds no key that opens it. The product as a whole is not end to end encrypted and no screen says it is. What is left visible is on the "What the host can see" screen and in `docs/SECURITY.md`.
- "So can the supplier read our records?" No. They see identifiers, how many people hold a key, the record type coarsely, the classification and roughly when work happens. Not who the person is and not what the record says.
- "Can anybody read them?" Yes, and deliberately. Two of five escrow holders, in two different organisations, with a purpose, a lawful basis, a written reason, a signature from each and a notification to the other three. If the answer were no, the council could not answer a subject access request or a sheriff's order, and the ICO treats data made unreadable by encryption as a security failure in its own right.
- "Does this stop somebody looking up their neighbour?" If they are not on the case, yes, and that is the point worth taking away. It does not stop somebody who is on the case from misusing what they see. Nothing cryptographic does, and the Security page says so rather than implying otherwise.
- "What if a laptop is stolen?" The key sits in the operating system keychain and the local copy is encrypted under it. The user revokes the device themselves. Cached cases stay readable until the offline grace period runs out, seeded at 72 hours, because a social worker in a house with no signal still has to open the safety plan. That window is the trade and it is configuration.
- "What happens to the child's views?" They are recorded with who took them and how, shown first on the dashboard, read into the meeting record by the chair, and printed in the minutes pack.
