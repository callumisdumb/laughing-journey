# Records management

How every entity in the product comes into being, changes, and reaches its terminal state. One row per entity in `docs/DATA-MODEL.md`, including the entities with no create path, where the row says why.

This is the specification for the records work, and it is written before that work rather than after it. Where a cell says something the product does not yet do, that is the specification talking; `docs/HANDOVER.md` carries what is built.

## The rules the matrix rests on

**Nothing in casework is deleted.** A record that has been shared cannot be unshared by deleting it, and the audit trail of what people believed and when is frequently the point of the record. The vocabulary is *correct*, *close*, *end* and *recorded in error*, never *delete*. The only deletion in the product is a user removing something from a draft they have not yet saved.

**Search precedes create, for people.** There is no direct create path for a `Person`. Two records for one child is how information fails to join up, and in a multi-agency system the two records are held by different agencies who each believe they have the whole picture. The path is search, review candidates, then create only after asserting that none match, and that assertion is recorded on the new record.

**Every write goes through one pipeline.** Validation, audit, classification, rewrapping, exclusion check, clock triggers, chronology event where the change is significant, sharing records and the notifications the change implies (who is told, derived from the record before and after and from the matrix, never composed by a screen: D-207), connector proposal, persistence. Ten steps, one implementation, asserted by a test that walks every source file under `apps/web` and fails on a write that does not go through it (`apps/web/lib/write.test.ts`). The raw persist is private to `store.ts`. The two other writers are `store.receive`, the connector delivery path, which carries the schema check and de-duplication and none of a person's consequences, because a person has not acted yet, and the clock engine, which writes notifications and the escalation marker on an overdue action and nothing else (D-208). The alternative is what the functionality audit found: 29 mutation sites each deciding for themselves.

**Significant event, or not.** A chronology event is written where the change is a significant event and not otherwise, because a chronology that records every corrected typo is a chronology nobody reads. The list is in section 3 of this document, so the distinction is a decision rather than an accident.

## The matrix

Read across: **C** create, **U** update, **T** terminal state, **X** correct, **→** triggers, **K** encryption.

## 3. What counts as a significant event

A chronology event is written for these and not for anything else. The list is short on purpose: a chronology that records every field edit is a chronology a practitioner scrolls past.

**Written**: a process opening, a stage change, a closure or a reopening; a meeting held and a minute distributed; a plan agreed or reviewed; a risk assessment recorded; a protection order applied for, granted or expiring; a household change or an address move; a relationship beginning or ending where it bears on a case; a death; a person merge; a views record; a disclosure decision; a break-glass access.

**Not written**: a typo corrected; a contact number changed; a status moved between queued and sent; a classification override, which is audited and shown on the record but is not a fact about the person; any change to configuration.

## 4. The flows that are more than an edit

**Opening a process.** Two gates, both giving reasons: eligibility from the person, permission from the persona (D-135). Every process is listed with its answer, including the ones that are not available. MAPPA has no age floor and warns rather than refusing under 18 (D-136); a 16 or 17 year old is offered adult support and protection and child protection at once, and the choice is recorded. An open case of the same type is shown first and a second one takes a reason; a MARAC referral runs the repeat check and sets the flag. Opening writes the reference, the opening stage and its history entry, the classification from the derivation rules, the case-role register, the notifications with their lawful basis, a chronology milestone on every subject, the audit entry, and the clocks that the *trigger* starts, which is fewer than it looks: only the two ASP clocks run from a concern (D-137). The dialog says which start and, where none do, what will start them.

**Creating a person.** There is no direct create. The sequence is search, review candidates, then create only if nothing matches, and the create button is not on the screen until the practitioner has explicitly said that none of the candidates is the person. That assertion is recorded on the new record as `createdAfterReviewing`, a count of the candidates that were on screen when it was dismissed, and editing any search field afterwards sends the flow back to the start so the number always describes the search that was actually reviewed. The duplicate search runs over every person in the product, including the ones the searcher has no access to: a candidate held behind need-to-know is shown as a name, a date of birth and a sentence saying the person may already be known and to ask for access, because the invisible record is the one that produces the duplicate. Candidates come back with the reason each one matched rather than a score. The implementation is `packages/domain/src/people/duplicates.ts` and the reasons are listed in D-116.

**Merge.** Two person records become one. Every relationship, process membership, chronology event, meeting, plan and sharing record repoints to the survivor; the audit ledger does not, because it records what happened. The retired record's name is kept as an alias on the survivor so an inbound connector event carrying the old reference still lands. Requires a reason of at least a sentence, is audited, and writes to the survivor's chronology. **It is reversible**, because conflating two children is worse than the duplicate it was meant to fix and it does happen: `packages/domain/src/people/merge.ts` keeps both records whole and the dotted path of every reference it moved, so the unmerge sets exactly those back. An undone merge keeps its record and is marked undone rather than deleted. See D-122 to D-127.

**Recording a death.** Not a checkbox. It closes every open process the person is a **subject** of, with the reason that process's own return uses: `criteria-no-opportunity` for ASP, which is the workbook's own row for a death during the process; `child-died` for child protection; de-registration for MAPPA. A process the person is only a **party** to is flagged for review and not closed, because a father dying does not close his child's child protection case: it changes it, sometimes profoundly, and that needs a person to read it rather than a system to shut it. The dialog lists every consequence before the button, computed by the function that performs them. Each closure is its own write, so each has its own audit entry, chronology entry and notifications. `packages/domain/src/people/death.ts`, D-155.

**Closing a process.** Stops the clocks and records why they stopped; writes the closure reason from the list that process's own national return uses; notifies contributing agencies at the detail level the matrix specifies; writes a chronology milestone; and proposes the outbound write that closes the episode in the source system. A case closed here and left open in the source system is exactly the divergence the reconciliation screen exists to catch.

The reason lists are not interchangeable. Child protection closes on the de-registration reasons from Children's Social Work Statistics; ASP on the actions-taken rows from the data workbook; MAPPA on the three exit kinds already in its schema. **MARAC and AWI have no national closure list this project has found**, so they are offered a locally agreed one which the dialog labels as local, and nothing chosen from it goes into a national return. `TODO(verify)` in `packages/domain/src/processes/close.ts`. The coded reason is written where the return reads it, not only into the prose field: a closure that only wrote prose would leave the quarterly figure to be reconstructed from it (D-150, D-151).

**Recording a decision.** The only way a case changes stage (D-211). The stage engine's table for the process type says what the current stage carries; the store checks the actor's role, then what the record must already hold, then the form's own validation, and refuses each in its own words before anything is written. Applying it writes the stage history entry, the detail the decision carries, the clocks it completes (with a note) and starts (each trigger an instant, validated by step 1 of the pipeline, D-226), the recorder onto the membership (D-219), the chronology milestone, the notifications to every member and matrix audience at their level, and then the follow-ons in order: a meeting scheduled, a plan and its actions, information requests to agencies, a closure, a linked case opened under the transition's authority (D-224), or a response written onto the request that asked (D-222). A follow-on that fails is a failed transition.

**Holding a meeting.** The close button on the workspace opens the outcome form of the transition the meeting's type fires from the case's stage, and recording it marks the meeting held with the transition as the reason (D-213). A type with nothing to fire is held with its minute opened and nothing decided. An inquorate planning meeting keeps the case where it is and starts the reconvene clock. The invite list, the people left off and the reason for each are on the meeting from scheduling (D-214), and every invitee is told of the invitation, a change and a cancellation.

**Asking to be involved.** A request is a record with the requester, their reason and the decision (D-227). It reaches the lead as a notification; anybody on the case decides it from the case; the requester is told; acceptance writes the membership with the reason.

**Reopening a process.** Takes a reason, is audited, and **resumes** the clocks the closure stopped rather than restarting them: the case was shut for a fortnight and the statutory period was running the whole time, so the deadline did not move. A deadline that was genuinely met before the closure stays met. `stoppedByClosure` on the trigger is what keeps the two apart (D-152). The case returns to the stage it was actually at, read from its own stage history.

**Recording something in error.** The terminal state for a record that should never have existed. It is not a deletion: the record stays, keeps its audit entries and stays in any pack already distributed. Working views stop showing it, and every view that still does says who decided and why. The chronology filters retired entries out of its list and counts them underneath it, because a list carrying them misleads a reader who scrolls it and a list that quietly dropped them misleads them differently (D-153). Reachable from a record's history, and from the toast that announced a create, which is the correction path with a shortcut rather than an undo (D-156).

**Correcting an identity field.** A change to a name, a date of birth or a CHI number goes through as a correction rather than an edit and requires a reason, because those are what other agencies match on: a match that worked yesterday fails today, and without a reason there is nothing on the record to say what happened. A telephone number does not, because it changed because it changed (D-154). Every change of either kind lands in the record's version history, which the write pipeline computes by diffing rather than trusting the screen to declare (D-148, D-149).

**Ending a relationship that is the basis of an exclusion.** Requires an explicit decision about whether the exclusion stands, recorded on the case, defaulting to *stands*. A former partner is frequently the whole risk, and silently un-excluding them is the most dangerous single thing in this matrix. Implemented so that the ending cannot lift it by accident: the party register reads the relationship record rather than its dates, so the exclusion survives the ending on its own. Confirming that it stands moves the entry from derived to explicitly recorded, with a name, a date and a reason; lifting it sets `stands: false` on an explicit entry, which is the only thing in the product that suppresses one. See D-132.

**Changing a household.** Adding somebody or recording that they left names the open processes anybody in the household is a subject of, and offers to tell the people the need-to-know matrix entitles, generating one sharing record each with the lawful basis the matrix names. Membership is dated: removing somebody sets an end date and a reason rather than deleting them, so who lived where and when survives (D-128). Both write a `household.change` chronology entry on everybody moved.

**Transferring a case to another authority.** Not a closure and not a stage like the others (D-248). The case moves to `transferred` from any non-terminal stage, naming the receiving area and the person there holding it. Every running clock completes with the reason, the status becomes `transferred`, the record says where it went, the case leaves the worklists and the engine offers nothing further, a second transfer included. Nothing is sent, because there is no route into another authority's system: the transfer records a handover made elsewhere, and the dialog says so before the button.

**Correcting a referral.** The opening record of an open case is corrected, never edited (D-245). `referralFields(type)` names what is correctable for each of the five types, and the subject and the perpetrator are not among them: those are changed on the case-party register and by a merge respectively, which the dialog says. A reason is required, the version history keeps both readings, and the chronology carries the correction. A correction that changes nothing is refused.

**Marking a return as submitted.** The product produces the file and sends nothing. A person sends it and records that they did (D-247): the recipient, the route, the date and any reference that came back. The deadline clock the period carries completes with it, a second submission for the same period is refused, and a date in the future is refused. It has its own audit act and its own target type.

**The life of a guardianship order.** An order is not a fact with a date and nothing after it (D-252). Four transitions record what happens to it: renewed to a new expiry and flagged as a renewal, because the Mental Welfare Commission counts renewals apart from new orders; varied, which keeps the powers it had before on the record; recalled, which ends it before its expiry and stops the renewal clock; and appealed, which is recorded whatever becomes of the appeal and changes no powers by itself. Each entry joins the order's `lifecycle`, and the renewal clock is recomputed from whichever expiry is current.

**Opening a Large Scale Investigation.** One adult's inquiry becomes an investigation into a setting (D-253), carrying the provider, the service type, the registration identifiers the national return asks for, the agencies involved and the chair. The chair must be a senior officer of the council, which the national minimum dataset glossary expects, and the record refuses an investigation whose chair is not one. Adults inside it are added one at a time as strands, each with their own concern and lead, because the return counts the investigation once and the adults in it separately. A strand about nobody is refused.

**Being away.** Nothing leaves the product: no email, no text, no push (D-254). What being away changes is who is told inside it. Name the dates and who is covering you, and each personal notification you would have had is written a second time to them, keyed on yours, so it renders at their own level and passes the same admissibility check. A role-addressed notification is never copied, because it already reaches whoever holds the role. The copies start and stop with the dates.

## 5. Guardrails

Every record created carries `synthetic: true` automatically, and the person create form carries a permanent line reminding the user that real personal data must not be entered. That line is not decoration: this will be shown to a room where somebody will be tempted to type in a live case, and the product should discourage it at the point of entry.

No fabricated identifiers. Generated CHI numbers follow the synthetic rules from the brief and are marked as synthetic in the interface, not only in the data.

Permission is checked at the action, not at the submit button: a user who cannot create a thing does not see a form they cannot submit, and the gate explains itself and offers the referral route.

No path creates a record without an audit entry, asserted by test.
