# Research log
Accessed 2026-09-02 unless stated.

Method note. In this session the network egress proxy refused direct fetches (HTTP 403 on CONNECT) to gov.scot, legislation.gov.uk, safelives.org.uk, scotland.police.uk and every council and NHS board domain tried. Every finding below therefore comes from search-result extracts of the named pages rather than from reading the page itself. The URLs recorded are the ones that appeared in the search results. Where a quotation is given it is the wording returned in the extract; treat it as accurate to the page but re-check against the live page when the network allows. Confidence ratings take this into account: High means the figure is stated in national guidance or statute and the extract quoted it directly.

## 1. Statutory and local clocks

### 1.1 MAPPA National Guidance: current edition
- Source: https://www.gov.scot/publications/scottish-government-multi-agency-public-protection-arrangements-mappa-national-guidance/
- Accessed: 2026-09-02
- Finding: The current edition is the 2022 refresh, published 31 March 2022 by the Justice Directorate (ISBN 9781804350645), described as "updated ministerial guidance to Responsible Authorities on the discharge of their obligations under sections 10 and 11 of the Management of Offenders etc. (Scotland) Act 2005" and as a "2022 refresh of the national guidance" that "was of existing MAPPA National Guidance last published in 2016". An errata was published on 25 April 2022 (hyperlink and data table corrections only). No version number was visible in the extracts, so cite it as "MAPPA National Guidance 2022". The National Overview Report 2024-2025 (published November 2025) says "A decision has been taken to proceed with updates to National Guidance where agreement has been established while continuing work where agreement is outstanding", so a further revision is in progress but not yet published.
- Confidence: High for edition and year; Verify for any version label.

### 1.2 MAPPA Level 2 review interval
- Source: https://www.gov.scot/publications/scottish-government-multi-agency-public-protection-arrangements-mappa-national-guidance/pages/5/ (chapter 4, The Assessment and Management of Risk) and https://www.gov.scot/publications/scottish-government-multi-agency-public-protection-arrangements-mappa-national-guidance/pages/20/ (chapter 19, Performance Management)
- Accessed: 2026-09-02
- Finding: "MAPPA Level 2 cases must be reviewed no less than once every 12 weeks". The guidance adds that the frequency of meetings "is a matter for the Lead Authority in liaison with the MAPPA Co-ordinator and partner agencies", informed by the risks and any significant change. Chapter 19 carries the matching national performance indicator: "95% of MAPPA Level 2 cases should be reviewed no less than once every twelve weeks".
- Confidence: High

### 1.3 MAPPA Level 3 (MAPPP) review interval
- Source: https://www.gov.scot/publications/scottish-government-multi-agency-public-protection-arrangements-mappa-national-guidance/pages/5/
- Accessed: 2026-09-02
- Finding: "MAPPA Level 3 cases must be reviewed no less than once every 6 weeks". Same Lead Authority discretion wording as 1.2 applies; 6 weeks is the maximum interval, not a fixed cadence.
- Confidence: High

### 1.4 Child protection: initial CPPM
- Source: https://www.gov.scot/publications/national-guidance-child-protection-scotland-2021-updated-2023/pages/12/ (Appendix D: Timescales for stages in child protection processes) and Part 3 at https://www.gov.scot/publications/national-guidance-child-protection-scotland-2021-updated-2023/pages/7/
- Accessed: 2026-09-02
- Finding: "If a child protection investigation occurs, a CPPM will follow within 28 calendar days of the concern being raised unless there is an IRD decision that this is not required." Exceptions "must be agreed by the accountable senior manager within the statutory social work service, who must be satisfied that an interim safety plan is in place". Appendix D also gives 5 calendar days notice of a CPPM to participants "wherever possible". Note the trigger wording: the clock runs from the concern being raised, not from the IRD.
- Confidence: High

### 1.5 Child protection: first core group after the CPPM
- Source: https://www.gov.scot/publications/national-guidance-child-protection-scotland-2021-updated-2023/pages/12/
- Accessed: 2026-09-02
- Finding: Appendix D, in its question and answer form, gives "How soon should a Core Group meet after a CPPM? Within 15 working days". Two separate extracts returned "15 working days"; none returned "15 calendar days". The brief seeds 15 calendar days, so the seed unit should change to working days. Appendix D also states that the core group must refer significant changes or concerns to the CPPM chair or lead professional "always within 3 calendar days of the change/concern being identified".
- Confidence: High for the figure; the unit (working days) is stated in the extract and should be confirmed against the live page.

### 1.6 Child protection: first review CPPM
- Source: https://www.gov.scot/publications/national-guidance-child-protection-scotland-2021-updated-2023/pages/12/
- Accessed: 2026-09-02
- Finding: "Within 6 months of the initial CPPM and thereafter 6 monthly or earlier if circumstances change significantly." The national guidance does not set a 3 month first review. The brief seeds 3 months for the first review; that figure is a local practice in some areas (older Edinburgh and the Lothians procedures used it) rather than the national figure. Recommended: seed 6 months as the national value and treat 3 months as a local override.
- Confidence: High for 6 months (national). The 3 month seed is Local.

### 1.8 Child protection: pre-birth CPPM timing
- Source: https://www.gov.scot/publications/national-guidance-child-protection-scotland-2021-updated-2023/pages/12/ and consultation report https://www.gov.scot/publications/national-guidance-child-protection-scotland-2021-consultation-report/pages/17/
- Accessed: 2026-09-02
- Finding: Appendix D sets the pre-birth CPPM "within 28 calendar days of concern being raised and within 28 weeks of gestation. There may be exceptions where the pregnancy is in the very early stages." The review of a pre-birth CPPM is "Within 3 months of a pre-birth CPPM but there should be latitude for professional judgement about the most appropriate timing post-birth." The guidance frames the clock against gestation (28 weeks), not against the expected date of delivery, so the product should store the gestation date or EDD and derive the 28 week point. The consultation report records support for holding the pre-birth meeting "as soon as possible and not wait for the 28 weeks".
- Confidence: High

### 1.9 ASP Code of Practice (July 2022): national timescales
- Source: https://www.gov.scot/publications/adult-support-protection-scotland-act-2007-code-practice-3/ (landing page), chapter 5 at https://www.gov.scot/publications/adult-support-protection-scotland-act-2007-code-practice-3/pages/7/, chapter 8 at https://www.gov.scot/publications/adult-support-protection-scotland-act-2007-code-practice-3/pages/10/, PDF at https://www.gov.scot/binaries/content/documents/govscot/publications/advice-and-guidance/2022/07/adult-support-protection-scotland-act-2007-code-practice-3/documents/adult-support-protection-scotland-act-2007-code-practice/adult-support-protection-scotland-act-2007-code-practice/govscot:document/adult-support-protection-scotland-act-2007-code-practice.pdf
- Accessed: 2026-09-02
- Finding: The Code does not set a national number of days for an inquiry decision or for holding a case conference. The extracts give: "local procedures should concentrate on the identification of reasonable and proportionate timescales for undertaking inquiries and assessments", "Local procedures should identify indicative timescales for completion of identified tasks", and that any protection plan "should include timescales for addressing risks and providing services". Case conference content (minutes, attendance, agreed plan with "anticipated timetable and reporting arrangements") is described but not clocked.
- Confidence: High that the Code leaves timescales to local procedures.

### 1.10 ASP local procedures: inquiry decision
- Source: West of Scotland Adult Support and Protection Practice Guidance, hosted by Argyll and Bute Council at https://www.argyll-bute.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2024-02/west_of_scotland_guidance_final_version.pdf, by Inverclyde Council (2019 version) at https://www.inverclyde.gov.uk/assets/attach/5198/West-of-Scotland-Guidance-Final-Version-2019.pdf and by South Ayrshire HSCP at https://hscp.south-ayrshire.gov.uk/media/2430/West-of-Scotland-Adult-Support-and-Protection-Guidance/pdf/West_of_Scotland_Adult_Support_and_Protection_Guidance.pdf?m=637630833588800000; Edinburgh Inter-agency Adult Support and Protection Procedure 2024 at https://www.edinburgh.gov.uk/downloads/file/35923/interagency-adult-support-and-protection-procedure; Fife Inter-Agency Adult Support and Protection Guidance at https://www.fife.gov.uk/__data/assets/pdf_file/0039/677793/ASPC-Interagency-Guidance-Non-Highlighted-Copy.pdf
- Accessed: 2026-09-02
- Finding: West of Scotland guidance (used by Argyll and Bute, Inverclyde, South Ayrshire, Renfrewshire and other west partnerships): "Social Work Services have 5 working days to complete an inquiry". Edinburgh 2024 procedure (live from 9 September 2024): the inquiry stage "should be completed within five working days". Fife: no explicit inquiry decision figure was visible; Fife clocks the council officer report "within 10 working days of the start of the investigation" and, for large scale investigations, an IRD "within 10 working days". The seed of 5 working days matches the West of Scotland and Edinburgh figures.
- Confidence: Local (examples: Argyll and Bute, Inverclyde, South Ayrshire via West of Scotland guidance, 5 working days; City of Edinburgh, 5 working days; Fife, 10 working days to report on investigation).

### 1.11 ASP local procedures: initial case conference
- Source: NHS Highland local ASP procedures at https://www.nhshighland.scot.nhs.uk/staff-and-partners/adult-social-care-for-staff/adult-protection/local-asp-procedures/ and https://www.nhshighland.scot.nhs.uk/media/4gmfrizc/lp-cf-recording-process-and-timescales.pdf; Renfrewshire HSCP ASP Operational Procedures December 2019 at https://www.renfrewshire.hscp.scot/media/11297/Renfrewshire-HSCP-ASP-Operational-Procedures-Dec-2019/pdf/RHSPC_ASP_Operational_Procedures_Dec_2019_.pdf?m=1581341773503; Orkney Adult Support and Protection Procedures February 2022 at https://www.ohb.scot.nhs.uk/sites/default/files/publications/ORKNEY%20ADULT%20SUPPORT%20AND%20PROTECTION%20PROCEDURES%20(FEBRUARY%202022).pdf; Edinburgh 2024 procedure as in 1.10
- Accessed: 2026-09-02
- Finding: Highland: "A Case Conference should be held within 21 days of an Adult Concern being recorded (ASP1&2)"; the unit is not stated as working days, so treat as calendar days. Renfrewshire: "The investigation should normally be completed within 20 working days of the initial referral" and "Following conclusion of the inquiry, the Case Conference should be held within 5 working days of conclusion". Orkney: the process chart shows "the full 20-day process from reporting a concern to case conference". Edinburgh: the initial protection plan "must be produced by the Council Officer 4 days prior to the initial ASPCC"; no overall day count for the initial conference was visible in the extract. The seed of 21 calendar days from concern matches Highland; Orkney (20 days) and Renfrewshire (20 working days plus 5) are close alternatives.
- Confidence: Local (examples: Highland 21 days; Orkney 20 days; Renfrewshire 20 working days to complete investigation then 5 working days to conference).

### 1.13 MARAC: research request return window
- Source: SafeLives Marac Operating Protocol Checklist at https://safelives.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/Marac-operating-protocol-checklist.pdf; SafeLives Marac toolkit for children and young people's services at https://safelives.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/Marac-toolkit-for-children-and-young-peoples-services.doc; SafeLives Toolkit for Marac in Scotland (mirror) at https://www.dvawp.co.uk/sites/default/files/2020-05/SafeLives'%20Marac%20Toolkit%20%E2%80%93%20Scottish%20Version.pdf; Marac information sharing and operating protocol guidance for Scotland at https://safelives.org.uk/resources-library/marac-information-sharing-and-operating-protocol-guidance-scotland/; Renfrewshire MARAC Operating Protocol 2025 at https://www.renfrewshire.hscp.scot/media/17495/MARAC-Operating-Protocol/pdf/Renfrewshire_MARAC_Operating_Protocol_2025_ozbwsqv75f42.pdf?m=1760452318407 (located, not read)
- Accessed: 2026-09-02
- Finding: SafeLives does not fix a national return deadline. Its toolkit describes the case list being "received from the Marac Coordinator approximately eight working days prior to the meeting", after which each agency "fills out as much of the research form as possible and takes it to the meeting". The Scottish guidance states that "Expectations about the use of a common research form by agencies should be addressed in the MARAC Operating Protocol" and that research forms are internal documents shared verbally at the meeting. English protocols seen in the same search (Richmond) allow "at least 9 clear working days to research the cases". No Scottish local protocol extract gave a return deadline in days; Dumfries and Galloway holds its MARAC every four weeks, and Shetland uses the Highlands and Islands MARAC service. The seed of 5 working days before the meeting is a product choice within the range implied by an 8 working day circulation.
- Confidence: Local (examples with a visible figure are English; Scottish protocols located for Renfrewshire, Midlothian and Dumfries and Galloway but no day count visible).

### 1.14 MARAC: 12 month repeat definition
- Source: https://safelives.org.uk/definition-repeat-marac and the briefing at https://safelives.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/Repeat-Definition-A-Briefing-for-Maracs.pdf
- Accessed: 2026-09-02
- Finding: "SafeLives defines a 'repeat' as ANY instance of abuse between the same victim and perpetrator(s), within 12 months of the last referral to Marac. The individual act of abuse does not need to be 'criminal', violent or threatening but should be viewed within the context of a pattern of coercive and controlling behaviour."
- Confidence: High

### 1.15 MARAC: 12 month flag practice
- Source: https://safelives.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/Repeat-Definition-A-Briefing-for-Maracs.pdf and https://safelives.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/Marac-operating-protocol-checklist.pdf
- Accessed: 2026-09-02
- Finding: "A standard action from a Marac meeting is for all agencies to 'flag and tag' files and provide a mechanism for practitioners to identify when a person accessing their service has been a party to the Marac process in the last 12 months. Cases should be flagged and tagged for 12 months since the last referral." The operating protocol checklist expects the coordinator to explain "how they let partners know that the case has had 12 months since the last Marac and therefore would not be considered a repeat should another incident occur". The flag clock runs from the last referral, not from the meeting date.
- Confidence: High

### 1.16 DASH and DAQ question counts
- Source: SafeLives DASH risk checklist for Scotland at https://safelives.org.uk/resources-library/dash-risk-checklist-for-scotland/ and PDF at https://safelives.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/Dash-risk-checklist-for-Scotland.pdf; Scottish Government consultation analysis at https://www.gov.scot/publications/improving-multi-agency-risk-assessment-interventions-victims-domestic-abuse-analysis-consultation-responses/pages/6/; Police Scotland FOI 24-1989 at https://www.scotland.police.uk/access-to-information/freedom-of-information/disclosure-log/disclosure-log-2024/september/24-1989-domestic-abuse-dash-risk-assessment-form/
- Accessed: 2026-09-02
- Finding: SafeLives: "There are 24 questions within the Scottish DASH risk checklist". Scottish Government consultation analysis: Police Scotland "would welcome further discussion on the balance of their 27 questions compared to the 24 identical ones used by other areas"; the DAQ "is based on the DASH but includes three additional questions to support decision making in relation to child protection". A DAQ score of 14 or above is treated as high risk. The Police Scotland FOI page confirms the DAQ is the police tool but its wording was not visible in the extract.
- Confidence: High (24 and 27 both stated in primary sources).

### 1.17 AWI 2000 s57(4): MHO report period
- Source: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/asp/2000/4/section/57 (also https://www.legislation.gov.uk/asp/2000/4/section/57/data.htm); supporting local material at https://www.highland.gov.uk/download/downloads/id/6107/pack_2.pdf
- Accessed: 2026-09-02
- Finding: The extract of section 57(4) reads that where the applicant claims an interest in the personal welfare of the adult and is not the local authority, notice of the intention to apply is given to the chief social work officer, and "the report referred to in subsection (3)(b) shall be prepared by the chief social work officer or, as the case may be, the mental health officer, within 21 days of the date of the notice." Section 57(3)(b) is the MHO report on the appropriateness of the order and the suitability of the nominated guardian, based on an interview and assessment "not more than 30 days before the lodging of the application". The 21 day clock starts on the date of the notice to the CSWO.
- Confidence: High (statute). Re-read the live section when the network allows, because the extract is a paraphrase-quality rendering of the subsection.

### 1.18 AWI 2000 s57: interim guardianship order limits
- Source: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/asp/2000/4/part/6/crossheading/guardianship-orders; Adult Support and Protection (Scotland) Act 2007 s60 at https://www.legislation.gov.uk/asp/2007/10/section/60 and its explanatory notes at https://www.legislation.gov.uk/asp/2007/10/notes/division/2/2; AWI explanatory notes at https://www.legislation.gov.uk/asp/2000/4/notes/division/2/6
- Accessed: 2026-09-02
- Finding: An interim guardianship order lasts until a guardian is appointed, or "three months from the date of appointment", or earlier recall. Section 60(1)(d) of the 2007 Act amended section 57 so that "sheriffs may grant interim guardianships for 3 months (as previously) or for a longer period up to a maximum of 6 months"; the amended text allows "such longer period (not exceeding 6 months)". So the statutory ceiling for an interim order is 6 months, which is the point the brief's awi.interim.warning rule warns at. The brief cites "Expert Working Group concerns 2026" for that rule; nothing was found for that reference in this pass and it should be treated as unverified.
- Confidence: High for the 3 month default and 6 month maximum in statute; Verify for the Expert Working Group citation.

## 2. Terminology checks

### 2.1 Inter-agency Referral Discussion (IRD)
- Source: https://www.gov.scot/publications/national-guidance-child-protection-scotland-2021-updated-2023/pages/9/ (Appendix A: Glossary) and Part 3 at https://www.gov.scot/publications/national-guidance-child-protection-scotland-2021-updated-2023/pages/7/
- Accessed: 2026-09-02
- Finding: The 2021 guidance (updated 2023) uses "Inter-agency referral discussion (IRD)", defined as "the start of the formal process of information sharing, assessment, analysis and decision-making following reported concern about abuse or neglect of a child or young person under the age of 18 years, in relation to familial and non-familial concerns". Part 3 states an IRD "must be convened without undue delay". Local material still using "Initial Referral Discussion" was not confirmed in this pass; the 2014 guidance PDF (https://hub.careinspectorate.com/media/1280/national-guidance-for-child-protection-in-scotland-2014.pdf) is the place to check that older usage. Keep the label configurable per area as the brief already says.
- Confidence: High for the current term; Verify for the claim that 2014 used "Initial".

### 2.2 ViSOR to MAPPS
- Source: https://www.gov.scot/publications/multi-agency-public-protection-arrangements-mappa-scotland-national-overview-report-2024-2025/pages/3/ (National MAPPA Developments, report published November 2025); earlier reports at https://www.gov.scot/publications/multi-agency-public-protection-arrangements-mappa-scotland-national-overview-report-2023-24/pages/3/ and https://www.gov.scot/publications/multi-agency-public-protection-arrangements-mappa-scotland-national-overview-report-2022-2023/pages/3/; ViSOR chapter of the national guidance at https://www.gov.scot/publications/scottish-government-multi-agency-public-protection-arrangements-mappa-national-guidance/pages/18/
- Accessed: 2026-09-02
- Finding: Yes, ViSOR is being replaced. The Home Office is developing "the Multi-Agency Public Protection System (MAPPS)" with UK partners including Scotland's Responsible Authorities. In 2024-25 "the MAPPS programme has had to reset and realign its priorities", with early user preview testing and "phased data migration from ViSOR to MAPPS". Work is "progressing towards having a national implementation plan, addressing challenges in order to be operational by 2028". A Home Office led Scottish Assurance Group is in place, with Scottish governance to be considered "in early 2026". As of 2026-09-02 ViSOR remains the live system and the national guidance still refers to ViSOR (chapter 17).
- Confidence: High for the replacement and the 2028 target; the date is a programme target, not a statutory date.

### 2.3 Other terms confirmed
- Child Protection Planning Meeting (CPPM) and core group are the 2021 guidance terms (Appendix D uses both). Adult Support and Protection Case Conference is abbreviated ASPCC in the Edinburgh 2024 procedure. Police Scotland's tool is the Domestic Abuse Questions (DAQ); SafeLives' is the DASH risk checklist (Scotland version). MAPPA Level 3 meetings are Multi-Agency Public Protection Panels (MAPPP) in the national guidance. The Fife ASP guidance uses "Interagency Referral Discussion (IRD)" for adults as well, so the product should not assume IRD is a child protection only term.

### 5.13 Government Security Classification (MAPPA National Guidance, Annex 2)
- Source: Annex 2 of the MAPPA National Guidance (31 March 2022 edition), supplied verbatim by the product owner on 03 Sep 2026.
- Accessed: 2026-09-03 (supplied text, not fetched)
- Finding: all Scottish Government departments, non-departmental public bodies and agencies follow the UK Government Security Classification scheme, which has three levels: Official, Secret and Top Secret. A classification indicates the sensitivity of information in terms of the likely impact of compromise, loss or misuse, and the need to defend against different threats.
  - **Official** is the lowest level and covers the majority of information the public sector creates or processes, including routine business operations and services. Some of it could have damaging consequences if lost, stolen or published in the media, but it is not subject to a heightened threat profile. There is **no requirement to explicitly mark routine Official information**.
  - **Official-Sensitive** is a limited subset of Official information that could have more damaging consequences if lost, stolen or published. It **must be clearly marked** Official-Sensitive, and special handling instructions may be added where the sensitivity justifies strict restrictions on sharing.
  - Annex 2 gives four examples of Official-Sensitive: case details involving vulnerable individuals where a serious risk of harm or criminal activity may result from disclosure; information about investigations, civil or criminal proceedings that could disrupt law enforcement or prejudice legal cases; security information; and personal data defined as special category or sensitive under data protection legislation.
  - Chapter 11 adds that the Minute and the Risk Management Plan are always Official and may be Official-Sensitive, and that an agency cannot share them widely with its personnel unless the chair of the MAPPA meeting has agreed.
- Confidence: High for the three levels, the "no requirement to mark routine Official" rule and the four examples; Verify the handling instruction descriptors against the organisation's own information security policy, because descriptor practice varies by organisation and has changed across editions of the UK scheme.

### 5.15 RESTRICTED is not a classification, and has not been one since 2014
- Source: read by the product owner on 03 September 2026 and quoted in the review that produced this change; corroborated by Annex 2 of the MAPPA National Guidance (2022) already recorded in 5.13, which names three levels and no fourth.
- Accessed: 2026-09-03 (supplied by the product owner)
- Finding: RESTRICTED was abolished on 2 April 2014, when the six-tier Government Protective Marking Scheme (UNCLASSIFIED, PROTECT, RESTRICTED, CONFIDENTIAL, SECRET, TOP SECRET) was replaced by the three-level Government Security Classification scheme. Annex 2 names exactly three levels, Official, Secret and Top Secret, with Official-Sensitive as a marking applied to a subset of Official rather than as a fourth level.
- Consequence for the product: the stored classification enum carried `restricted` as a third value, which was both a retired marking and a conflation of two orthogonal properties. It is split into a `Classification` (`level`, `sensitive`, `handling`) and an `AccessRestriction` (`none`, `restricted`); see D-073 and D-074. The glossary carries an entry recording the abolition, and another distinguishing the three unrelated senses in which the word restricted appears in this codebase.
- Confidence: High.

## 8. Writing into partner systems (04 Sep 2026)

Reading a source system and writing to one are different questions with different answers, and the second answer is usually "less than you think". The ceilings below are what a third party product would realistically be permitted to write, which is not the same as what the vendor's API technically exposes.

| System | Write ceiling | Basis | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| ECLIPSE (Civica), Mosaic, Liquidlogic, Swift | Full two-way | Same data controller as the platform's host: the council. Episode creation, stage change, allocation, closure. | High |
| CareFirst (OLM) | Batch only | Legacy and no longer under active development. A scheduled file exchange is what a partnership would actually get. | Medium |
| SEEMIS | Wellbeing flag and named-person alert | Education management information system. Writing narrative into a pupil record from outside is not a realistic ask. | Medium |
| EMIS Web | Coded flag plus a task or document, **after accreditation** | The EMIS Partner Programme and its transaction interface do permit writes, gated behind accreditation covering security, privacy and clinical safety, with a named Clinical Safety Officer, a hazard log, penetration testing and information governance sign-off. Never free clinical narrative authored by a non-clinician. | `TODO(verify)` |
| TrakCare, Morse | Alert only | | Medium |
| iVPD | **Notify only, not write** | Police Scotland internal system. A third party product writing into a vulnerable persons database is not something a police information governance lead would agree to. Outbound is modelled as a notification into a police queue carrying a reference. | High |
| ViSOR, MAPPS | **Never** | The reference is held and read. Nothing is written. | High |
| OPG | None | Lookup only in both directions. | High |
| SCRA | Referral submission | A referral to the Reporter is a legitimate outbound artefact with its own form. | Medium |

**The EMIS route is the one marked for verification and it matters which way.** The NHS England IM1 pairing route is an England programme with its own commercial and technical framework. The Scottish route runs through the EMIS Partner Programme and national contracting arrangements, and this project has not confirmed its current shape. The product therefore states the ceiling as unverified on the connector card rather than asserting a route. Nothing in the product depends on the answer: the ceiling is configuration, and a partnership that has the accreditation raises it (D-157).

The wider point is a commercial one. Anybody who has run an integration programme has watched a supplier claim everything writes and then discover, eighteen months in, that the police system was never going to accept a write. Saying iVPD is notify-only in the first minute buys more credibility than it costs.
