# Scenario 1: Marion Fraser, 79, Portlennan

## The story

Marion has lived at 14 Shore Loan since her marriage in 1971 and has been a widow since 2019. Her GP, Dr Amira Farouk, disclosed vascular dementia in November 2025; her capacity fluctuates and she is clearer in the mornings. Her nephew Callum Fraser (38) lives in Auchentorran, visits several times a week, does her shopping, and since about June 2026 has been staying some nights in her spare room. He holds her bank card under an unregistered "arrangement" that nobody wrote down. The OPG register shows no power of attorney and no guardianship, though the GP recommended one twice.

On 20 August 2026 Clydeshore Savings Bank raised a vulnerable customer concern with Police Scotland: 31 cash machine withdrawals totalling £2,410 in ten weeks, most of them at Auchentorran, several late in the evening. Marion had come into the branch unsure why her balance was low. The Adult Concern Report reached the council on 21 August. The three-point test was met on all three limbs, the inquiry decision to investigate was made on working day three, and the investigation opened on 27 August with an inter-agency discussion. Moira Gilmour (council officer) and Stuart Blair (second worker) made the s7 visit and s8 interview on 28 August with Marion's advocate Tam Guthrie present. A s10 records request went to the GP and has been returned. Undue pressure from the nephew was considered and found likely. A banning order application is being drafted for the case conference on 11 September, nine days from the demo date.

A linked AWI process holds the capacity question: Dr Farouk will assess Marion's capacity for financial decisions on 4 September, and the OPG result is on file. Marion's own words are recorded: she wants to stay at home, she is fond of Callum and does not want him "in trouble", and she wants her money back.

1. Sign in as Moira Gilmour (Clydeshore HSCP, council officer). Home shows the initial case conference clock at 9 days, the overdue action to move Marion's pension to a new account, and one pending connector event.
2. Open People, search "Marion". Open Person record. Read the header: age, address since 1971, no alerts, an ASP badge at investigation and an AWI badge at capacity concern, both with next dates. The drawer shows who is involved by agency and why.
3. Open Marion's views. Her words from 28 August sit at the top: stay at home, keep Callum out of trouble, get the money back, and her preference that Callum is not told what she said until Moira has spoken to him.
4. Open the Chronology tab, then "Open wide". 7. 10. Sign in as Alistair Meek (OPG) and open the same record: named fields only. Sign in as Dr Farouk: the s10 request and her own returns, not the police narrative.

## What it proves

- ASP to AWI linkage: one adult, two processes, linked both ways, with the capacity question carried in the investigation record.
- s10 records request to a GP shown as a named-fields share to the records holder.
- Advocacy offered and accepted, and the adult's stated wish to stay at home recorded in her own words with her sharing preference respected.
- Undue pressure considered and recorded with reasoning, and protection orders considered one by one.
- Two ASP clocks from one trigger (inquiry decision, case conference), with the local source of each shown.
- A connector event promoted into the integrated chronology and one still waiting.
- Need-to-know by role: OPG sees fields, the GP sees health and the s10 request, police narrative stays agency-only.
