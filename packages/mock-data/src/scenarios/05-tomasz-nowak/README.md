# Scenario 5: Tomasz Nowak, 44, Dunlarrick

## The story

Tomasz has held the tenancy of a ground floor flat at 3 Distillery Vennel since 2016. He is Polish, reads English well, and asks for a Polish interpreter for anything that matters. He has had depression since 2019, treated by Dr Amira Farouk, and stopped his medication in the summer of 2025. He keeps twenty years of Polish newspapers stacked floor to ceiling in his living room and hall. Housing sent two tenancy warning letters in 2025, both in English.

On 29 May 2026 a neighbour called 999 when his smoke alarm kept sounding (burnt toast). Scottish Fire and Rescue offered a home fire safety visit, and on 5 June Gordon Nairn found a blocked rear exit, paper within half a metre of the cooker and one working alarm. He fitted alarms and raised an adult concern the same afternoon. The three-point test was met: he cannot make his home safe without help, the fire risk is serious, and his depression makes him more vulnerable. The inquiry decision to investigate was made on working day five, an inter-agency discussion on 16 June planned the visit with an interpreter, and Moira Gilmour and Stuart Blair made the s7 visit and s8 interview on 19 June in Polish. Dr Farouk assessed on 24 June that he has capacity for decisions about his home. Undue pressure was considered and not found. He consented to the inquiry and declined intervention.

The case conference on 9 July, which he attended with his interpreter and advocate Tam Guthrie, decided that he is an adult at risk, that no protection plan is needed because he has capacity and declines, and that a support-only response should be agreed with his consent: alarms, a clear route to the door, weekly visits from a Polish-speaking worker, a GP review of his mood, and housing enforcement paused. Fire and rescue's dissent about the residual risk is recorded. The review conference is on 8 October.

## What it proves

- The adult's right to refuse: capacity assessed, undue pressure considered and not found, refusal respected and recorded.
- The least restrictive principle: every protection order considered and rejected with a reason.
- A support plan that is not a protection plan, with the adult's consent recorded and a route to withdraw.
- Interpreter and communication needs carried into visits, meetings, the minute and the plan.
- Dissent recorded against a decision without blocking it, and carried to the review agenda.
- A clock override with the reason and the national position visible.
- The referrer receives an outcome, not the record.
