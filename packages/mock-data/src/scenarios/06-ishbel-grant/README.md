# Scenario 6: Ishbel Grant, 84, Clydeshore Royal Infirmary

## The story

Ishbel has lived at 22 Larch Brae, Dunlarrick since 1975 and has been a widow since 2014. Alzheimer's type dementia was diagnosed in 2023; power of attorney was recommended twice and never granted. Care at home started in 2024 and was increased to three visits a day in April 2026 after her son reported night-time wandering. She has fallen three times in sixteen months, each time found later than the last. On 15 July 2026 the morning carer found her at the foot of the stairs with a fractured wrist. She has been on ward 7 since, medically fit for discharge since 24 July.

Heather Aitken, the discharge coordinator, raised a capacity concern on 28 July. Dr Ruth Cameron, consultant geriatrician, assessed on 4 August that Ishbel lacks capacity for the decision about where she lives; Dr Amira Farouk assessed on 6 August that she lacks capacity for financial decisions. The OPG register shows no attorney and no guardian. Stuart Blair recorded her will and preferences on 7 August: she told her daughter for years that she never wanted to go into a home, and she says now "I want to go home to my own bed". Her daughter Morag Kilgour supports residential care. Her son Douglas Grant objects and offers to move in.

Because Douglas objects, section 13ZA of the 1968 Act cannot be used, and the route decision on 12 August was welfare guardianship with the council as applicant, powers limited to residence, care and information. The multi-disciplinary meeting on 20 August agreed to apply, to seek an interim order, and not to discharge before the order or an agreement; Douglas's dissent is recorded against each decision. Graeme Dunlop, MHO, was notified on 24 August, which started the 21-day report clock: due 14 September, 12 days from the demo date. Both medical reports are in. The application was lodged at Ardvale Sheriff Court on 28 August with a motion for an interim order, not yet granted. The hearing is on 25 September.

1. Sign in as Graeme Dunlop (Clydeshore HSCP, Mental Health Officer). Home shows the MHO report clock at 12 days with its source (AWI 2000 s57(4)) and his report task due 14 September.
2. Open People, search "Ishbel". Open Person 360. The header shows the inpatient alert (ward 7, delayed discharge), the home address and the hospital address period with its note, the communication needs (hearing aids, mid-morning, short sentences), and the AWI badge at application with the hearing date.
3. Open her views: her own words from 7 August at the top, then the family views from 20 August with both children quoted.
4. Open the Chronology tab. Read the falls: May 2025 found at once, March 2026 a long lie, July 2026 a fracture. Read the GP lane: diagnosis, power of attorney recommended twice. Switch to Stuart's analysis note on the falls. The two capacity assessments, the OPG check, the route decision and the court lodgement follow in order.
5. Open the inbox: a pending TrakCare ward note from 1 September (she tried to leave the ward at 03:20, second episode this week). Promote it with the AWI lawful basis.
6. Open Processes, then the AWI dashboard: stage stepper at application; the MHO clock; two capacity assessments, decision-specific, with evidence and communication support; will and preferences with past wishes, present wishes and who was consulted; the OPG result; the route decision with the s13ZA record and the objection named; the application panel with powers sought, MHO report in progress, two medical reports received, no suitability report, court lodged 28 August, hearing 25 September, interim order sought and not granted.

8. Open Actions: the MHO report task owned by Graeme Dunlop, the discharge planning task owned by Heather Aitken, and the open task to meet Douglas with the council solicitor.
9. Sign in as Jean Hogg (MWC): summary only. Sign in as Heather Aitken: the concern she raised, the discharge decisions and her own actions.

## What it proves

- The AWI workflow end to end: capacity concern, existing powers, route decision, application, with the order stage still to come.
- Capacity as decision-specific and time-specific: two assessments, two decisions, two assessors.
- Will and preferences recorded before the route decision, and family consultation recorded with disagreement intact.
- s13ZA considered and rejected with the reasoning and the objector named.
- The statutory MHO report clock from the date of notice, with the source and days remaining.
- Medical report tracking, court timeline and interim order status in one place.
- A person with a home address and a current hospital location, shown without losing either.
