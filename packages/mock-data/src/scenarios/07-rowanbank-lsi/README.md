# Scenario 7: Rowanbank Care Home, Large Scale Investigation

## The story

Rowanbank Care Home at 1 Rowan Gait, Braeside is run by Rowanbank Care (Scotland) Ltd. Six of its residents, aged 78 to 92, are the subjects of one Adult Support and Protection process: Wilma Sneddon (92), Jean Kilgour (88, Alzheimer's dementia, with a welfare and financial attorney), Malcolm Rankin (86, type 1 diabetes), Zofia Wisniewska (84, Polish, needs an interpreter), Margaret Cairns (80, Parkinson's disease) and Archie Drummond (78, vascular dementia).

On 28 and 29 July 2026 the Care Inspectorate carried out an unannounced inspection and found seventeen medication administration errors on the July MAR charts: omitted doses, warfarin given twice in a day, time-critical Parkinson's medication given hours late. Fourteen of the errors were on the 20:00 round, and one agency nurse was the only registered nurse on duty for most of them. Three residents had already come to harm: an emergency department attendance with an INR of 5.8, a GP call-out for high blood glucose with ketones, and a fall. The Care Inspectorate sent an adult concern to the council on 30 July. Moira Gilmour applied the three-point test at setting level the same day and opened an inquiry on 31 July.

On 5 August a second concern arrived from a different direction: Linda Paterson, Wilma Sneddon's daughter, reported five cash withdrawals from her mother's personal allowance account that nobody had asked for, each signed with the same initials. That is the financial strand, and it brought in the police and the Office of the Public Guardian.

The LSI planning meeting on 6 August decided to proceed as a Large Scale Investigation with a strand per resident, a lead worker for each (Moira Gilmour or Stuart Blair), a parallel Care Inspectorate inspection, a police investigation of the financial allegation, and a pause on new placements, which the provider's operations director dissented from by name. The investigation opened on 7 August: two section 7 visits, three section 8 interviews (one with a Polish interpreter) and one refusal, records under section 10 from the home and the GP practice, capacity assessed for each resident (two lack capacity for this decision), undue pressure considered and not found, and advocacy accepted. No protection order was needed. The practice pharmacist's extended audit and Dr Farouk's analysis show the errors cluster on one shift pattern. The review of strands is on 10 September; one commissioning action is overdue.

## What it proves

- One process with many subjects: subjectIds, strands and a household that is a care home, with per-resident consent, capacity and views.
- The ASP investigation powers as records: s7 visits, s8 interviews including a refusal, s10 records with the health-professional rule, s9 not arranged, orders considered and not required.
- The joint chronology mixing setting-level and per-resident events, with analysis kept separate from facts.
- Need-to-know by strand and role: police and OPG on fields for the financial strand, the provider on decisions and actions only, a withheld request from the provider recorded as a sharing record.
- The duty to cooperate (s5) in action: Care Inspectorate, health, police, OPG and advocacy on one distribution list, with a commissioning lead and the home manager named as invitees without platform accounts.
- Clocks as configuration: the inquiry decision on working day 5, and the initial case conference clock overridden with a recorded reason because the LSI planning meeting stands in its place.
- A human-in-the-loop connector inbox with two pending EMIS Web medication reviews.
