# Scenario 8: Chloe Reid, 19, pregnant, Ardvale

## The story

Chloe lives at 31 Meadow Loan, Ardvale, a tenancy in her sole name that she got as a care leaver in February 2024. She is 27 weeks pregnant with a due date of 27 November 2026. Her partner Jordan Blake (23) moved in around May 2026. He is known to police for domestic abuse of a previous partner: a concern report in 2024, an assault that November, a conviction in March 2025 with an 18 month non-harassment order, and an alleged breach of that order in August 2025.

Chloe's own record is a child's record first. In February 2019, aged 12, she was found alone with no food while her mother lay drunk on the floor. There was an IRD, a comprehensive medical, registration under neglect and parental substance use (CP-2019-0088), and a children's hearing that placed her with her aunt Sheena Reid under a compulsory supervision order. She was de-registered in November 2020 because the placement held. It broke down in June 2021 when her aunt fell ill, and she moved to foster carers in Craiglarrick. She left care at 16, went through supported accommodation, got her own flat, and ended throughcare support at 18 saying she did not need a worker. By 2025 she had nobody.

She booked late, at 15 weeks, with Jordan in the room, and answered no to the routine domestic abuse enquiry. On 9 August 2026 police attended the flat after neighbours heard shouting; Jordan was drunk and Chloe said they had just argued. The concern hub passed the report to midwifery. On 18 August, seen alone by community midwife Kasia Nowicka, Chloe disclosed that Jordan had grabbed her wrist, keeps her bank card, checks her phone and frightens her when he drinks. Kasia raised a pre-birth child protection concern that afternoon.

Two processes now run, linked to each other and to the closed 2019 record:

- **CP-2026-0447** (stage: investigation). Pre-birth IRD held 20 August with social work, police, the child protection nurse adviser, midwifery and health visiting. The unborn baby is judged likely to be at risk of significant harm after birth. JII and medical recorded as not applicable with reasons; no emergency measures; Reporter referral considered and not made because the child is unborn; Jordan told of the concern but not of the police information. The pre-birth CPPM clock is overridden to 4 September, the day Chloe reaches 28 weeks, which comes before the 28 calendar day point. The CPPM is scheduled for 4 September with David Laird in the chair; midwifery and police reports are back, health visiting and GP are still to come.
- **MARAC-2026-0101** (stage: referral). Referral from midwifery received 26 August after a DASH of 15 out of 24. Not a repeat. Sadia Qureshi is the IDAA and has recorded Chloe's wishes. Research requests have not yet gone out. A DSDAS Power to Tell disclosure was made to Chloe on 31 August.

Chloe's own words, recorded by her social worker after the IRD: "I want this baby to have what I did not have."

## Demo path

1. Sign in as Janet Kerr (social worker). Home shows the pre-birth CPPM clock at two days with its override reason, and the housing and health visitor actions due this week.
2. Open People, search "Reid". Two records: Chloe Reid and "Unborn baby Reid" with an expected delivery date instead of a date of birth. Open the unborn baby's Person 360: the relationship "unborn child of Chloe Reid", the CP badge, the household.
3. Open Chloe's Person 360. Her hero shows her as an adult subject of a MARAC and, in the same header, the mother in a pre-birth CP process. Scroll the address history: five addresses, each with a reason. The closed 2019 CP process sits in her process list with its register dates.
4. Open the chronology wide. The care lane runs from the 2019 concern report through two placements to the end of throughcare; the health lane picks up with the late booking and the disclosure; the police lane shows the 9 August report. Read Janet Kerr's analysis note in its own lane: the isolation, not the history, is the risk.
5. Open the CP dashboard: the IRD record with each agency's contribution, seven decisions including the two "not applicable" ones with their reasons and the midwife's dissent on when Jordan was told, the interim safety plan, and the clock panel showing the national 28 day rule alongside the 28 week override.
6. Open the pre-birth CPPM in Meetings: the pack builder shows the midwifery and police reports returned and two still outstanding, the DASH summary, Chloe's views, and the note that Jordan will be seen separately by the chair.
7. Sign in as Kasia Nowicka (midwife). She sees the IRD record because the subject is an unborn baby (the need-to-know row says so), her own action, and the pre-meeting request addressed to her. She does not see the MARAC research that has not yet started.
8. Sign in as Sadia Qureshi (IDAA). Open the MARAC at referral stage: DASH with 24 questions, the referral summary noting the pregnancy, the DSDAS note, and the link to the CP process. Jordan Blake is not a recipient of anything; the drawer explains why.
9. Open the inbox: a TrakCare maternity encounter from 1 September (reduced fetal movements, CTG normal, seen alone for part of it) is waiting to be promoted or dismissed.

## What it proves

- Unborn-baby handling: a person record with an expected delivery date, an "unborn child of" relationship, a pre-birth CP process with its own detail block and a meeting type of its own.
- The pre-birth CPPM clock as configuration: the 28 day rule is visible and the 28 week gestation cap is applied as a due date override with a reason.
- An adult who is the subject of one process (MARAC) and the parent in another (pre-birth CP) at the same time, with the closed process from her own childhood linked but not used against her.
- IRD decisions recorded even when the answer is "not applicable", with rationale and dissent.
- The DASH as a 24 question record, distinct from the 27 question police DAQ.
- Midwifery in the room for an unborn baby, and health visiting on the pre-birth pathway before there is a child.
- Perpetrator exclusion from need-to-know and the record of what was withheld from him and why.
